# Educlub Progressive Scratch Pathway Design

## Decision

Educlub will provide three progressive Scratch courses rather than one broad
course:

1. **Scratch Explorer: Creative Coding Foundations**
2. **Scratch Creator: Games, STEAM and Smart Systems**
3. **Scratch Innovator: Advanced Computing and Responsible AI**

Each course contains 10 modules. Each module supports a 90-minute facilitated
club session, 2-3 projects or project variants, and additional self-learning
activities. School administrators decide which course is appropriate for each
learner. Educlub does not automatically enforce an age or placement rule.

The pathway is designed for learners aged 7-14, mixed-ability groups, rolling
enrolment, classrooms, clubs, and independent learning.

## Source Synthesis

The pathway draws different strengths from the three supplied PDFs:

- **Learn to Program with Scratch** supplies the computer-science progression:
  motion, drawing, procedures, variables, decisions, loops, strings, lists,
  search, sorting, mathematical modelling, and scientific simulation.
- **Scratch Programming in Easy Steps** supplies the child-friendly delivery
  pattern: clear visual steps, quick visible results, progressively larger
  projects, games, quizzes, music, sensors, and encouragement to customize.
- **50+ STEM Activities for Any Primary Classroom** supplies authentic project
  contexts: space, flight, animal movement, habitats, design challenges,
  measurement, data, scheduling, enterprise, and practical investigation.

The PDFs are references for curriculum analysis and inspiration. Educlub course
text, project briefs, artwork, diagrams, questions, and examples must be
original. The course must not reproduce book pages, screenshots, or substantial
passages.

## Recommended Pedagogy

The pathway uses a **spiral, project-based STEAM model**.

Concepts return at increasing depth. For example, learners first use a variable
as a score, later use several variables to model a system, and finally use
variables and lists to represent data for an AI-style decision system. This is
more suitable than a topic-by-topic textbook sequence because it supports
mixed ages, visible progress, creativity, and repeated practice.

Every module follows this learning cycle:

1. **Imagine:** Meet a real-world challenge and predict how a solution might
   work.
2. **Learn:** Explore one or two key coding or STEAM concepts.
3. **Plan:** Write an algorithm, draw a flowchart, storyboard, or design sketch.
4. **Build:** Complete a guided mini-project.
5. **Create:** Choose or adapt a main project.
6. **Test:** Use a checklist, collect evidence, and debug.
7. **Explain:** Describe how the project works and why decisions were made.
8. **Extend:** Complete optional Try It Yourself or AI/STEAM challenges.
9. **Reflect:** Submit work and identify the next improvement.

## Module Activity Pattern

The existing Educlub activity types will be used. A module may contain several
activities without forcing every heading into a separate activity.

Required module components:

- module launch, objectives, vocabulary, and visual guide;
- unplugged prediction, design, or algorithm activity;
- guided Scratch skill lab;
- 2-3 projects or project variants;
- one STEM or STEAM connection;
- one debugging or testing activity;
- five-question knowledge check with hints and explanations;
- reflection and `.sb3` submission;
- optional Try It Yourself activities for independent learning;
- teacher notes, support strategies, and extension guidance.

A normal 90-minute club session should use approximately:

- 10 minutes: hook, demonstration, and prediction;
- 15 minutes: concept and guided skill;
- 45 minutes: project build;
- 10 minutes: testing and peer feedback;
- 10 minutes: quiz, reflection, saving, and submission.

Longer projects can continue through self-learning without preventing learners
from completing the core module experience.

## Differentiation

Every main project offers three paths:

- **Explorer Path:** starter assets, worked steps, fewer required features, and
  vocabulary support.
- **Creator Path:** standard success criteria with meaningful design choices.
- **Innovator Path:** deeper logic, data, user experience, or AI/STEAM
  extensions.

These paths describe support and challenge, not fixed age groups. Learners can
move between paths by module. Completion is based on demonstrated outcomes,
not the chosen path.

## Course 1: Scratch Explorer

**Subtitle:** Creative Coding Foundations

**Purpose:** Build confidence, creativity, foundational computational thinking,
and safe independent learning.

**Typical readiness:** New Scratch learners or learners who need a structured
foundation.

**Portfolio outcome:** Stories, animations, art, music, games, quizzes, and a
simple STEM design project.

### Module 1: Welcome to Creative Coding

**Concepts:** Scratch workspace, sprites, stage, events, sequence, safe saving.

**Projects:**

1. Make a Character Come Alive
2. My Name in Motion
3. Try It Yourself: Create a welcome card for Educlub

**Future skill:** Giving precise instructions to computers and AI systems.

### Module 2: Story Studio

**Concepts:** Backdrops, costumes, dialogue, timing, broadcasts, storyboards.

**Projects:**

1. Two-Character Conversation
2. A Story with Three Scenes
3. Try It Yourself: Add a player choice or alternate ending

**STEAM link:** Language, performance, visual storytelling, and sequencing.

### Module 3: Animation and Music Lab

**Concepts:** Costume animation, sound, rhythm, parallel scripts, synchronization.

**Projects:**

1. Walking or Dancing Character
2. Digital Music Performance
3. Try It Yourself: Animate a cultural celebration or original song

**STEAM link:** Music patterns, tempo, animation, and performance design.

### Module 4: Shapes, Patterns and Digital Art

**Concepts:** Coordinates, direction, repeat loops, angles, pen extension.

**Projects:**

1. Shape Drawing Machine
2. Repeating Pattern or Mandala
3. Try It Yourself: Design a textile, tile, or nature-inspired pattern

**STEAM link:** Geometry, art, symmetry, and pattern recognition.

### Module 5: Maze Mission

**Concepts:** Keyboard input, sensing, conditionals, collision, coordinates.

**Projects:**

1. Guide a Sprite Through a Maze
2. Moon Rover Rescue
3. Try It Yourself: Add hazards, checkpoints, or another level

**STEM link:** Rover design, navigation, constraints, and resilient solutions.

### Module 6: Catch, Score and Improve

**Concepts:** Variables, random positions, loops, score, timer, feedback.

**Projects:**

1. Catch the Falling Objects
2. Healthy Food Collector or Clean-Ocean Game
3. Try It Yourself: Add bonus and penalty objects

**STEM link:** Classification, environmental choices, counting, and probability.

### Module 7: Quiz Maker

**Concepts:** Ask and answer, operators, conditionals, score, feedback.

**Projects:**

1. Three-Question Quiz
2. Maths, Science, or School Knowledge Challenge
3. Try It Yourself: Add a timer and different feedback messages

**Future skill:** Comparing user input with expected answers.

### Module 8: Nature in Motion

**Concepts:** Direction, repetition, simple rules, broadcasts, multiple sprites.

**Projects:**

1. Migrating Birds Animation
2. Move Together Without Colliding
3. Try It Yourself: Change speed, group size, or obstacles and observe results

**STEM link:** Migration, animal movement, coordination, and observation.

### Module 9: Build, Test, Improve

**Concepts:** Engineering design cycle, variables, measurement, fair tests.

**Projects:**

1. Paper Aircraft Test Recorder
2. Digital Landing Challenge
3. Try It Yourself: Compare two designs using a chart or score

**STEM link:** Forces, flight, measurement, evidence, and iteration.

### Module 10: Explorer Showcase

**Concepts:** Planning, decomposition, debugging, presentation, reflection.

**Projects:**

1. Remix and Improve a Previous Project
2. Original Story, Game, Art, Music, or STEM Project
3. Try It Yourself: Create instructions so another learner can use the project

**Portfolio evidence:** Plan, `.sb3` file, test checklist, explanation, and
reflection.

## Course 2: Scratch Creator

**Subtitle:** Games, STEAM and Smart Systems

**Purpose:** Develop stronger programming knowledge through substantial games,
simulations, data projects, and creative design.

**Typical readiness:** Learners who can independently use events, sequences,
loops, basic conditionals, and simple variables.

**Portfolio outcome:** Multi-level games, simulations, interactive systems, and
an introduction to machine learning through classification.

### Module 1: Interactive Story Director

**Concepts:** Broadcasts, state, user choices, nested decisions, scene control.

**Projects:**

1. Branching Adventure
2. Interactive Public-Service Story
3. Try It Yourself: Create three endings and track player choices

### Module 2: Advanced Maze Designer

**Concepts:** Collision correction, timers, lives, level state, reusable logic.

**Projects:**

1. Multi-Level Maze
2. Lunar Landing Site Challenge
3. Try It Yourself: Add an enemy, key, locked gate, or accessibility mode

### Module 3: Arcade Game Systems

**Concepts:** Variables, clones, random spawning, difficulty, game states.

**Projects:**

1. Clone Catch or Avoid Game
2. Space Defence Challenge
3. Try It Yourself: Add enemy types and progressive difficulty

### Module 4: Procedures and Drawing Machines

**Concepts:** Custom blocks, parameters, decomposition, nested loops.

**Projects:**

1. Polygon Factory
2. Generative Art Studio
3. Try It Yourself: Create reusable blocks for spirals, flowers, or symbols

### Module 5: Smart Pet Simulator

**Concepts:** State variables, gradual change, boundaries, decision rules.

**Projects:**

1. Virtual Pet with Needs
2. Habitat Care Simulator
3. Try It Yourself: Add weather, health, day/night, or consequences

**STEM link:** Systems, needs, habitats, and cause and effect.

### Module 6: Forces, Flight and Motion

**Concepts:** Speed, direction, gravity models, input variables, measurement.

**Projects:**

1. Free-Fall or Projectile Simulator
2. Glider Design Comparison
3. Try It Yourself: Graph or report how one changed variable affects motion

### Module 7: Ecosystem and Climate Lab

**Concepts:** Clones, probability, populations, environmental variables, models.

**Projects:**

1. Predator, Food, and Population Model
2. Water, Weather, or Habitat Change Simulation
3. Try It Yourself: Run three trials and explain the differences

### Module 8: Lists, Data and Decisions

**Concepts:** Lists, collection, min/max, average, search, visual reporting.

**Projects:**

1. Class Survey and Bar Chart
2. Migration or Environmental Data Explorer
3. Try It Yourself: Build a recommendation or lookup tool

### Module 9: How Machines Learn

**Concepts:** Examples, labels, features, classification, confidence, bias.

**Projects:**

1. Build a Rule-Based Animal Classifier in Scratch
2. Train a Small Image, Pose, sound, or text classifier using a
   school-approved external tool and connect or recreate its decisions in
   Scratch
3. Try It Yourself: Test unusual examples and document mistakes

**AI responsibility:** Do not use faces or private learner data. Explain that a
model learns patterns from examples and can be confidently wrong.

### Module 10: Creator Design Challenge

**Concepts:** User needs, decomposition, prototypes, testing, iteration.

**Projects:**

1. Team STEAM Design Sprint
2. Original Game, Simulation, Smart System, or Data Project
3. Try It Yourself: Add an AI-inspired feature and explain its limitations

## Course 3: Scratch Innovator

**Subtitle:** Advanced Computing and Responsible AI

**Purpose:** Use Scratch as a serious computing laboratory for algorithms,
modelling, data, AI concepts, responsible design, and future technology.

**Typical readiness:** Learners who can create projects with variables, lists,
conditionals, broadcasts, clones, and custom blocks.

**Portfolio outcome:** Advanced algorithms, simulations, data systems,
AI-enabled experiments, and a socially useful capstone.

### Module 1: Software Designer Mindset

**Concepts:** Requirements, decomposition, abstraction, pseudocode, testing.

**Projects:**

1. Rebuild a Small Project from a Written Specification
2. Debugging Detective Challenge
3. Try It Yourself: Create a test plan and reusable project starter

### Module 2: Advanced Game Architecture

**Concepts:** State machines, level data, reusable procedures, clone management.

**Projects:**

1. Multi-Level Arcade Game
2. Strategy, Defence, or Resource Game
3. Try It Yourself: Add adaptive difficulty or controller options

### Module 3: Search, Sort and Recommendation

**Concepts:** Linear search, frequency, sorting, comparison, ranking.

**Projects:**

1. Visual Search and Sorting Lab
2. Rule-Based Book, Career, Activity, or Revision Recommender
3. Try It Yourself: Compare two algorithms for speed and clarity

### Module 4: Mathematical and Scientific Modelling

**Concepts:** Functions, parameters, recursion or nested repetition, graphs.

**Projects:**

1. Pattern, Fractal, or Growth Model
2. Motion, Orbit, Electricity, or Population Simulator
3. Try It Yourself: Validate the model against calculated or observed data

### Module 5: Engineering Control Systems

**Concepts:** Inputs, sensors, feedback, thresholds, outputs, control loops.

**Projects:**

1. Smart Greenhouse or Watering System Simulation
2. Line-Following or Obstacle-Avoiding Robot Simulation
3. Try It Yourself: Connect approved hardware or sensor extensions where
   available

### Module 6: Data Science Studio

**Concepts:** Data quality, lists, averages, median, outliers, charts, claims.

**Projects:**

1. Clean and Explore a Small Dataset
2. Interactive Community or School Data Story
3. Try It Yourself: Identify a misleading chart or unsupported conclusion

**Responsibility:** Use anonymous or public, teacher-approved data only.

### Module 7: Machine Learning Laboratory

**Concepts:** Training, testing, features, labels, confusion, confidence.

**Projects:**

1. Scratch Simulation of a Learning Classifier
2. Real Classifier Experiment with a school-approved AI tool or extension
3. Try It Yourself: Improve the training examples and compare results

**Required evidence:** Training plan, test cases, result table, known
limitations, and no sensitive personal data.

### Module 8: Generative AI and Creative Partnership

**Concepts:** Prompts, generation, iteration, hallucination, attribution,
human judgement.

**Projects:**

1. Compare Human and AI Project Ideas, then select and improve one
2. Use a school-approved generative tool to support brainstorming, dialogue,
   image concepts, sound concepts, or debugging, while keeping the learner as
   author
3. Try It Yourself: Verify three AI claims and record corrections

**AI responsibility:** Never enter private information. Review every output for
accuracy, stereotypes, unsafe content, and copyright concerns. Learners must
state where AI assisted their work.

### Module 9: AI for People and Planet

**Concepts:** Stakeholders, fairness, accessibility, sustainability, trade-offs.

**Projects:**

1. Audit a Fictional AI System for Bias and Harm
2. Prototype an AI-Inspired Solution for education, accessibility, health,
   agriculture, conservation, or community safety
3. Try It Yourself: Redesign the solution after stakeholder feedback

### Module 10: Future Innovator Capstone

**Concepts:** Research, design, implementation, evaluation, communication.

**Projects:**

1. Capstone Proposal and Prototype Review
2. Original Advanced Scratch or Scratch-plus-AI Solution
3. Try It Yourself: Produce a demo video, user guide, or exhibition poster

**Capstone evidence:** Problem statement, intended users, research, algorithm,
prototype, source acknowledgements, AI-use statement, testing evidence,
limitations, `.sb3` submission, and presentation.

## AI Learning Framework

AI is introduced progressively:

- **Explorer:** Algorithms, rules, inputs, outputs, patterns, and the difference
  between a person and a programmed character.
- **Creator:** Classification, training examples, labels, testing, errors, and
  simple real-tool experiments.
- **Innovator:** Machine-learning evaluation, generative AI, data quality,
  bias, privacy, attribution, human oversight, and social impact.

AI content must not be an isolated final lesson. Short "AI Connection" prompts
will appear throughout the pathway, such as comparing a maze rule with a robot
navigation rule or comparing a quiz answer check with classification.

Real AI activities are optional where school policy, connectivity, accounts,
or safeguarding requirements prevent access. Every activity must have a
Scratch-only simulation alternative that teaches the same underlying concept.

## Assessment Model

Each module includes:

- a five-question quiz with 80 percent mastery, hints, explanations, and
  retries;
- a project rubric covering function, coding, design, testing, explanation,
  and responsible technology use where relevant;
- a learner reflection;
- an `.sb3` submission;
- optional screenshots, test tables, plans, or short explanations;
- teacher feedback guidance.

Course completion should prioritize portfolios over quiz scores. Suggested
course weighting:

- projects and portfolio: 60 percent;
- planning, testing, and explanation: 20 percent;
- quizzes: 10 percent;
- reflection and participation: 10 percent.

## Rolling Enrolment and Placement

School administrators control allocation. Educlub may display recommended
readiness statements, but it must not automatically block a learner because of
age.

To support learners joining at different times:

- every course starts with a short orientation and saving/submission guide;
- every module opens with prerequisite reminders and a quick skill check;
- reusable help cards explain common Scratch operations;
- learners can revisit earlier activities;
- challenge work remains optional;
- teachers can assign support activities without changing course placement.

The courses remain progressive. Rolling enrolment means flexible allocation and
self-paced progress, not that modules have no learning sequence.

## Engagement and Inclusion

Projects should use varied contexts: stories, art, music, sport, space, nature,
community, enterprise, accessibility, games, and science. Learners should often
choose characters, themes, data, and presentation style.

Course content must:

- use child-friendly language and short instructions;
- include visual and text alternatives;
- avoid gendered assumptions and culture-specific knowledge as a requirement;
- work without public Scratch sharing or a Scratch account;
- avoid violent, frightening, or gambling-style reward designs;
- protect learner identity and private information;
- include accessible project options such as keyboard alternatives, readable
  contrast, instructions, captions, and adjustable speed where practical.

## Teacher Guidance

Each module contains:

- preparation and materials;
- concepts to demonstrate;
- discussion questions;
- common misconceptions and bugs;
- Explorer, Creator, and Innovator support;
- offline or low-connectivity alternatives;
- safeguarding and AI-use notes;
- success criteria and a concise rubric;
- optional links to related school subjects and careers.

Teachers facilitate investigation rather than demonstrate every block in the
finished project. Worked examples should teach the minimum skill needed, then
leave meaningful design choices to learners.

## Educlub Template Direction

The three courses will be independent editable built-in templates:

- `SCRATCH-EXPLORER`
- `SCRATCH-CREATOR`
- `SCRATCH-INNOVATOR`

Each template has exactly 10 ordered modules. The existing generic course
builder, quizzes, assignments, reflections, project submissions, adoption, and
template synchronization should be reused.

The previous `SCRATCH-INTERMEDIATE` design is superseded by this pathway and
should not be implemented as a fourth overlapping course. Useful content from
that design can be mapped primarily into Scratch Creator.

Before implementation, the template contract must determine whether every
project variant is represented as a separate Educlub `project` activity or as
choices inside one main project activity. The recommended default is:

- one guided mini-project activity;
- one required choice-based main project activity containing 2 project briefs;
- one optional Try It Yourself assignment;
- one reflection/submission activity.

This keeps module navigation manageable while still providing 2-3 builds.

## Visual Direction

Each course needs:

- one course cover;
- one pathway or roadmap image;
- one hero image per module;
- one original algorithm, model, or concept diagram per module;
- selected Scratch-style block diagrams where text alone is insufficient.

Visuals should use a consistent bright educational style while giving each
course a distinct identity:

- Explorer: playful warm colors and large simple forms;
- Creator: energetic blue-green palette and project-workshop scenes;
- Innovator: deep blue-violet palette with data, systems, and future-technology
  motifs.

Visuals must not copy the books, imitate the Scratch interface exactly, or rely
on protected characters. Every visual requires alternative text and a complete
text explanation.

## Validation and Testing

Automated template tests must verify:

- all three templates validate and import independently;
- every course contains exactly 10 modules;
- every module contains objectives, vocabulary, planning, practice, projects,
  quiz, reflection, submission, teacher notes, and differentiation;
- every quiz contains exactly five complete questions;
- every required project accepts `.sb3` submissions;
- every visual uses a valid local path and alternative text;
- AI modules contain privacy, safety, testing, and human-oversight guidance;
- adopted school copies preserve ordering, quizzes, projects, and media;
- previous built-in templates continue to validate and import;
- learner content remains usable on mobile and without images.

Manual curriculum review must confirm:

- complexity rises clearly across all three courses;
- projects are original and not copied from the PDFs;
- each module is achievable at core level in 90 minutes;
- Try It Yourself activities provide meaningful self-learning;
- real AI activities have no-account or Scratch-only alternatives;
- instructions are understandable to learners at the intended readiness level.

## Success Criteria

The pathway is successful when:

- Educlub offers three clearly differentiated Scratch courses;
- each course contains 10 complete project-based modules;
- learners can produce at least 20 meaningful builds across each course when
  project choices and extensions are included;
- STEM, STEAM, computational thinking, creativity, and communication are
  visible throughout;
- AI knowledge progresses from algorithms to responsible real-tool use;
- learners can work in a 90-minute club session and continue independently;
- school administrators retain placement control;
- learner portfolios show increasing independence, technical depth, testing,
  and social responsibility.

## Out of Scope

- automatic age-based course placement;
- unrestricted learner access to public generative AI tools;
- collection of biometric, facial, voice, or other sensitive training data;
- reproducing the supplied books as course content;
- embedding the full Scratch editor inside Educlub;
- automatically grading the internal logic of `.sb3` files;
- requiring public Scratch accounts or unmoderated project sharing.
